# Contributor Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

We, as members, contributors, and maintainers of DevHub, pledge to make
participation in our community a harassment-free experience for everyone,
regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex
characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience,
education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race,
religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming,
diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

We are committed to building a platform that prioritises the quality of
software and the honesty of feedback over popularity. That commitment begins
with the way we treat one another.

## Our Standards

### Behaviour That Contributes to a Positive Environment

Examples of behaviour that strengthens our community include:

- **Demonstrating empathy and kindness** toward other people.
- **Respecting differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences** — including
  disagreements about design, architecture, and priorities.
- **Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback.** Critique code, not
  people. Explain the reasoning behind a request.
- **Accepting responsibility** and apologising to those affected by our
  mistakes, and learning from the experience.
- **Focusing on what is best for the community** rather than on what is best
  for us individually.
- **Assuming good faith.** When intent is unclear, ask before concluding.
- **Being patient with newcomers.** Everyone was a beginner once.
- **Documenting decisions** so that future contributors can understand the
  reasoning, not just the outcome.

### Behaviour That Is Unacceptable

Examples of unacceptable behaviour include:

- **The use of sexualised language or imagery**, and unwelcome sexual attention
  or advances of any kind.
- **Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments**, and personal or political
  attacks.
- **Public or private harassment** of any kind.
- **Publishing others' private information**, such as a physical or email
  address, without their explicit permission (doxxing).
- **Deliberately misgendering** or using names that a person has asked you not
  to use.
- **Sustained disruption** of discussions, issues, or pull requests after
  being asked to stop.
- **Spam, self-promotion unrelated to the project**, or repeated off-topic
  comments.
- **Encouraging or failing to report** any of the above.
- **Other conduct** which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

### Additional Standards Specific to DevHub

Because DevHub is a curation platform, contributors are also expected to:

- **Review honestly.** Ratings and reviews must reflect genuine experience, not
  coordination with others, self-promotion, or retaliation.
- **Respect authorship.** Do not claim another person's work as your own, and
  do not link to projects in a way that misrepresents their origin or licence.
- **Respect licences.** Only share code or assets in a manner consistent with
  their original licence.
- **Do not weaponise moderation.** Reporting a contribution as a violation
  must be based on a good-faith assessment, not on disagreement with the
  contribution's technical merit.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to all project spaces, including:

- The GitHub repository — issues, pull requests, discussions, and code review.
- The DevHub platform itself — project pages, reviews, comments, and profile
  content.
- Communication channels associated with the project — email, chat, and any
  future community space.
- In-person or online events held under the project's name.

It also applies when an individual is **officially representing the project**
in public spaces. Examples of representing the project include using an
official email address, posting through an official social media account, or
acting as an appointed representative at an online or offline event.

## Enforcement Responsibilities

Project maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing the
standards of acceptable behaviour. They will take appropriate and fair
corrective action in response to any behaviour they deem inappropriate,
threatening, offensive, or harmful.

Maintainers have the right and responsibility to:

- **Remove, edit, or reject** comments, commits, code, issues, and other
  contributions that are not aligned with this Code of Conduct.
- **Communicate reasons for moderation decisions** when appropriate.
- **Temporarily or permanently restrict** participation for anyone whose
  behaviour violates these standards.

Maintainers who do not follow or enforce this Code of Conduct in good faith
may face temporary or permanent consequences as determined by other members
of the project's leadership.

## Reporting

### How to Report

If you experience or witness unacceptable behaviour, or have any other
concerns, please report it to the project maintainer as soon as possible.

**Reporting channels:**

| Channel          | Address                                                          | Notes                                                      |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Email**        | `conduct@example.com`                                            | Preferred for all reports.                                 |
| **GitHub**       | Direct message to [@Sobhan-SRZA](https://github.com/Sobhan-SRZA) | For reports that do not involve the maintainer themselves. |
| **Contact form** | `/contact`                                                       | If neither of the above is available.                      |

Do **not** use the public issue tracker or pull request comments for conduct
reports.

### What to Include

To help us respond effectively, please include:

1. **Your contact information**, so we can follow up.
2. **Names of the individuals involved**, or usernames and links if
   applicable.
3. **A description of the incident**, including what happened and when.
4. **A description of any prior related incidents**, if applicable.
5. **Any supporting material** — screenshots, links to messages, logs.
6. **Whether you would like to remain anonymous** to the reported party.

If you are reporting on behalf of someone else, please confirm that they
consent to the report being made.

### What Happens After a Report

1. **Acknowledgment.** All reports will be reviewed and investigated promptly
   and fairly. You will receive an acknowledgment within **48 hours**.
2. **Confidentiality.** The maintainer will respect the privacy and security of
   the reporter. Details of the incident will be shared only with those who
   need to know in order to resolve it.
3. **Investigation.** The maintainer will gather facts from all relevant
   parties. Both the reporter and the reported party will have an opportunity
   to present their account.
4. **Decision.** A determination will be made based on the evidence and the
   enforcement guidelines below.
5. **Communication.** The reporter will be informed of the outcome. If the
   report results in action against the reported party, that party will also
   be informed of the reason.

### Retaliation

**Retaliation against anyone who reports a violation in good faith is itself a
violation of this Code of Conduct.** Any contributor who retaliates — through
harassment, exclusion, professional harm, or otherwise — will be subject to
enforcement actions, up to and including a permanent ban.

## Enforcement Guidelines

Project maintainers will follow these guidelines in determining the
consequences of any action they deem in violation of this Code of Conduct.

The four tiers below are progressive. A first-time minor violation typically
results in a Correction. A repeat violation, or a single severe violation,
may jump directly to a Temporary or Permanent Ban.

### 1. Correction

**Community impact:** Use of inappropriate language or other behaviour deemed
unprofessional or unwelcome in the community.

**Consequence:** A private, written warning from the maintainer, providing
clarity around the nature of the violation and an explanation of why the
behaviour was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.

**Duration:** None. The warning remains on record.

### 2. Warning

**Community impact:** A violation through a single incident or series of
actions.

**Consequence:** A warning with consequences for continued behaviour. No
interaction with the people involved — including unsolicited interaction with
those enforcing the Code of Conduct — for a specified period of time. This
includes avoiding interactions in community spaces as well as external
channels such as social media.

Violating these terms may lead to a temporary or permanent ban.

**Duration:** Typically 7 to 30 days.

### 3. Temporary Ban

**Community impact:** A serious violation of community standards, including
sustained inappropriate behaviour.

**Consequence:** A temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public
communication with the community for a specified period of time. No public or
private interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited
interaction with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, is allowed during this
period.

Violating these terms may lead to a permanent ban.

**Duration:** Typically 30 to 90 days.

### 4. Permanent Ban

**Community impact:** Demonstrating a pattern of violation of community
standards, including sustained inappropriate behaviour, harassment of an
individual, or aggression toward or disparagement of classes of individuals.

**Consequence:** A permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within
the community. The individual's contributions may be removed, and any future
participation will not be accepted.

**Duration:** Permanent.

## Appeals

If you believe an enforcement decision was made in error, you may appeal it.

**How to appeal:**

1. Send a written appeal to `conduct@example.com` within **14 days** of
   receiving the enforcement decision.
2. State clearly which decision you are appealing and why.
3. Provide any additional information or context that was not considered in
   the original decision.

**What happens next:**

- Appeals will be reviewed by a maintainer who was **not** involved in the
  original decision, if one is available. If the project has only one active
  maintainer, appeals will be reviewed after a cooling-off period of 30 days.
- The review will focus on whether the process was followed correctly and
  whether the decision was proportionate to the violation. Appeals are not a
  re-litigation of the facts.
- You will receive a written decision within **30 days**.
- All appeal decisions are final.

## Summary

In short:

- **Be respectful.** Disagree about code freely; never attack the person.
- **Assume good faith.** Most conflicts are misunderstandings.
- **Report privately.** Do not escalate in public threads.
- **No retaliation.** Ever.
- **Honest feedback.** Ratings and reviews must reflect genuine experience.
- **Respect authorship and licences.** Give credit where it is due.

The project exists to help developers find and improve good software. That
work is only possible in a community where people feel safe contributing.
